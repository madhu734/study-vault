Binary Semantic Feature (BSF) Calibration: Diplomatic AI Scenario   [50]
Context: A tech company is developing an AI for diplomatic and corporate negotiations. The AI struggles to differentiate between the
titles 'king', 'president', 'CEO', and 'monarch', often using them interchangeably and causing diplomatic incidents.
Tasks:
a) Using the concepts of descriptive meaning and denotation, explain why these terms are conceptually distinct despite sometimes
referring to a single individual.
b) Propose a set of binary semantic features to distinguish these four lexemes: [±HUMAN], [±ADULT], [±HEREDITARY],
[±GOVERNMENTAL], [±CORPORATE], [±HEAD OF STATE]. Complete a feature matrix using '+' for presence, '-' for absence, or
blank if not applicable.
c) Evaluate the efficacy and limitations of this feature-based approach in unravelling complex semantic structures.

Categorization Debate: Botanist vs. Chef (Tomato Case)   [50]
Scenario: A botanist states: "Based on its structure, developing from the flower's ovary and containing seeds, a tomato is
unequivocally a fruit."
The chef retorts: "In my world, it's a vegetable. Its flavour profile is savoury, not sweet, and it belongs with onions, not apples."
Tasks:
a) Analyse the botanist's argument using the traditional Necessary and Sufficient Conditions (NSC) model of categorisation.
b) Analyse the chef's argument using Prototype Theory. Explain how a tomato can be understood as a marginal, non-prototypical
FRUIT, yet a prototypical VEGETABLE.
c) Using Wittgenstein's concept of family resemblance, explain how tomatoes, carrots, and spinach all belong to the category
VEGETABLE without a single shared property.
Gricean Maxim Flouting in an Interview Context   [50]
Scenario: An interviewer asks a job candidate: "So, what did you think of your last boss?"
The candidate, who strongly disliked their previous boss, replies: "She was very punctual."
Questions: Which of Grice's conversational maxims is the candidate flouting (Manner, Quantity, Relation, or Quality), and what is the
conversational implicature generated?
Corporate Memo Ambiguity & Disambiguation Scenario   [49]
Memo: "All staff must complete their annual performance reviews by Friday. Delays may lead to consequences, including potential
demotions, reassignment of duties, or other actions at the discretion of management."
Tasks:
a) Identify and explain different types of ambiguity (lexical, syntactic, temporal) in the memo.
b) Discuss homonymy vs. polysemy regarding terms like 'consequences' and 'duties'.
c) Explain disambiguation techniques management should apply to make communication precise.
d) Analyze the role of the Principle of Consistent Interpretation and context in resolving meaning shifts.
