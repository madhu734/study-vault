### 1. Robert Frost

1. **Examine Frost's treatment of metaphors in his poems.**  
   `[Batch: 50]`

2. **How do Robert Frost's poetic choices shape his approaches to the human condition and the natural world? Discuss.**  
   `[Batch: 49]`

3. **"Life is shown in Robert Frost's poems to contain many aspects of darkness, which often seem concatenated in a series of depressions of the human spirit." Discuss this statement with reference to the poems that you have read.**  
   `[Batch: 48]`

4. **In many poems of Robert Frost there is a recurrent use of the Keatsean notion of "negative capability". Discuss with reference to the poems that you have read.**  
   `[Batch: 47]`

5. **Write a note on Robert Frost's poems that you have read and show how they deal with complex social and philosophical themes.**  
   `[Batch: 46]`

6. **Robert Frost's poetry reveals the paradoxes of human life. Discuss.**  
   `[Batch: 44]`

7. **Robert Frost's poems explore the relationship between humanity and nature. — Do you agree? Explain.**

### 2. Sylvia Plath

1. **Analyse Sylvia Plath's poetic techniques with reference to "Lady Lazarus" and "Ariel".**  
   `[Batch: 50]`

2. **How does Sylvia Plath's poetry interrogate the boundaries between victimhood and agency? In "Daddy" and "Lady Lazarus", how does the speaker's transformation from suffering to rage reflect agency?**  
   `[Batch: 49]`

### 3. Eugene O'Neill: Long Day's Journey into Night

1. **In Long Day's Journey into Night, the characters struggle with inner anguish and use different psychological defence mechanisms to escape their past. Discuss with reference to the text.**  
   `[Batch: 50]`

2. **In what ways do Long Day's Journey into Night and Death of a Salesman depict the impact of family conflict on individual self-identity?**  
   `[Batch: 49]`

3. **"In Long Day's Journey into Night, all characters are obsessed with their past guilt." Justify this statement.**  
   `[Batches: 48, 46]`

4. **Discuss Long Day's Journey into Night as a modern tragedy.**  
   `[Batch: 47]`

5. **Mary represents the Tyrones' pathetic obsession with their past. Do you agree? Justify your answer.**  
   `[Batch: 44]`

6. **The Tyrones in Long Day's Journey into Night are stuck in their (concept of) home and bewildered at the same time in their intensifying sense of homelessness. — Explain.**  
   `[Batch: 43]`

7. **"None of us can help the things that life has done to us. They're done before you realize it... and you've lost your true self forever." Do you think that this comment focuses on the principal theme of Long Day's Journey into Night?**  
   `[Batch: 42]`

   `[Batches: 50, 47]`

### 5. William Faulkner: As I Lay Dying

1. **In what ways does As I Lay Dying depict the breakdown of family unity? Discuss with reference to the text.**  
   `[Batch: 50]`

2. **Discuss the experimental narrative techniques and multi-perspectivity in the works of William Faulkner with reference to As I Lay Dying.**  
   `[Batch: 49]`

3. **In what ways can Darl be considered a tragic hero in As I Lay Dying? Elaborate.**  
   `[Batch: 48]`

4. **What is Cubism? Discuss As I Lay Dying as a Cubist novel.**  
   `[Batch: 47]`

5. **"The coffin is a burden to the Bundren family, and it confirms the dysfunctionality of the family structure." Elaborate this statement from your reading of As I Lay Dying.**  
   `[Batch: 46]`

6. **Evaluate Faulkner's technique and purpose of using multiple narrators in As I Lay Dying.**  
   `[Batch: 44]`

7. **Discuss the structure and the narrative technique of As I Lay Dying.**  
   `[Batch: 43]`

8. **Discuss Addie's interior monologue in As I Lay Dying. Why do you think it is placed in the middle of the narrative?**  
   `[Batch: 42]`

### 6. Ernest Hemingway: Short Stories

1. **Discuss the psychological, social and cultural dimensions of modern man's alienation as evident in Hemingway's short stories.**  
   `[Batch: 50]`

2. **"In most of his writings, Hemingway explores the absence of emotional intimacy in human relationships, addressing existential crisis and nihilism." Discuss this statement referring to the texts that you have read.**  
   `[Batch: 48]`

3. **What is Hemingway's Iceberg theory? How did Hemingway apply this technique in his writings? Refer to the texts that you have read.**  
   `[Batch: 47]`

4. **'Macomber's boyishness is contrasted with Wilson's strong masculinity.' Discuss this statement from your reading of "The Short Happy Life of Francis Macomber".**  
   `[Batch: 46]`

5. **How does Hemingway portray the existential crisis in his short stories? Discuss with reference to the stories you have read.**  
   `[Batches: 44, 43]`

6. **Hemingway's code hero is "a man who lives correctly, following the ideals of honour, courage, and endurance in a world that is sometimes chaotic, often stressful and always painful." — Illustrate.**  
   `[Batch: 42]`
