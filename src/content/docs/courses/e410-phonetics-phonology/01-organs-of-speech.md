# Organs of Speech & Articulation

## 1. The Three Airstream Systems

1. **Respiratory System (Lungs):**
   - Initiates the **pulmonic egressive** airstream mechanism, utilized by the vast majority of human languages including English and Bengali.
2. **Phonatory System (Larynx & Vocal Folds):**
   - **Voiced Sounds:** Vocal folds held loosely together, vibrating rapidly in the airstream (e.g., `/b/`, `/d/`, `/z/`, all English vowels).
   - **Voiceless Sounds:** Vocal folds wide apart, airstream passes without vibration (e.g., `/p/`, `/t/`, `/s/`).
3. **Articulatory System (Supraglottal Cavities):**
   - Pharynx, Oral Cavity, and Nasal Cavity acting as variable resonators.

---

## 2. Active vs. Passive Articulators

| Articulator | Role | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Active Articulators** | Moveable organs that make contact or constriction | Lower lip, Tongue (Tip, Blade, Front, Back, Root) |
| **Passive Articulators** | Immoveable surfaces against which active articulators press | Upper teeth, Alveolar ridge, Hard palate |

## 3. Quick-Fire Viva Questions

**Q1: What is the biological function vs. secondary function of the vocal cords?**  
> *Answer:* The biological function is acting as a valve to prevent food/foreign bodies from entering the lungs. The secondary (derived) evolutionary function is phonation (speech sound production).

**Q2: Which sounds in English are articulated with the soft palate (velum) lowered?**  
> *Answer:* Nasal consonants: `/m/` (bilabial), `/n/` (alveolar), and `/ŋ/` (velar).
