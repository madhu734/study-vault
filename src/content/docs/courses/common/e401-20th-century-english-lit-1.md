# E 401: 20th Century English Literature I

## 🎯 Course Objective & Context
This course samples major works of seven British and Irish writers of the 20th century. Students will situate literary pieces against the socio-political background of 20th-century UK and significant modernist movements:
- **Modernism & Postmodernism**
- **Surrealism**
- **Regionalism**
- **Postcolonialism**
- **Theatre of the Absurd**

---

### Module 2: Drama

#### George Bernard Shaw (1856–1950)
- ***Pygmalion*** (Themes: Social class, phonetics, language as social determinant, transformation)

#### Samuel Beckett (1906–1989)
- ***Waiting for Godot*** (Themes: Theatre of the Absurd, existentialism, cyclic time, paralysis)

---

### Module 3: Fiction

#### Joseph Conrad (1857–1924)
- ***Heart of Darkness*** (Themes: Imperialism, psychological journey, the hollow core of civilization)

#### David Herbert Lawrence (1885–1930)
- ***Sons and Lovers*** (Themes: Industrialism, Oedipal conflict, working-class life, alienation)

#### James Joyce (1882–1941)
- ***A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man*** (Themes: Stream of consciousness, epiphanies, aesthetic theory)

---

## 📝 Critical Focus & Exam Questions
1. **Modernist Poetic Sensibility:** Contrast between Yeats's symbolic myth-making (*Spiritus Mundi*) and Eliot's fragmentation (*objective correlative* in *The Waste Land*).
2. **Absurdism vs. Social Realism:** Language breakdown and Beckett's existential waiting vs. Shaw's dialectical realism in *Pygmalion*.
3. **The Modernist Novel:** Stream of consciousness and subjective narrative in Joyce vs. psychological realism in Lawrence.
