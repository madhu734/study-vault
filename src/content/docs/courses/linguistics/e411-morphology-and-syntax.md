# E 411: Morphology and Syntax

## 🎯 Course Objective
Studies two core structural layers of language: **Morphology** (basic word forms, word formation) and **Syntax** (sentence formation, hierarchical constituency).

---

## 🧩 Part 1: Morphology

### 1. Introduction to Morphology
- Scope and communicative functions of morphology; morpheme definition

### 2. Words and Lexemes
- Word vs. Lexeme vs. Word-form
- Empirical tests for wordhood (cohesion, mobility, pauses)
- Simple, complex, and compound words
- Morphemes: Free (lexical, functional) vs. Bound (derivational, inflectional)
- Roots, Bases, and Stems
- **Word Formation Processes:** Affixation, Compounding, Conversion, Clipping, Blending, Back-formation, Acronyms, Coinage, Reduplication

### 3. Morphological Analysis
- Morphological trees and structural segmentation
- Resolving morphological ambiguities
- Syntagmatic and paradigmatic relations

### 4. Morphological Variations
- Morphs, Allomorphs, Conditioning (phonological, morphological, lexical)
- **Prosodic Morphology, Zero Morph & Suppletion**

### 5. Morphological Interfaces
- Morphophonology, Morphosyntax, and Morphosemantics

---

## 🌳 Part 2: Syntax

### 1. Syntactic Structure & Word Classes
- Syntactic categories and word classes: N, V, Adj, Adv, Prep, Det, Conj
- Linear order vs. Hierarchical constituency

### 2. Sentence Constituents & Phrases
- Constituency tests: *Substitution, movement, clefting, stand-alone, coordination*
- Phrase types: NP, VP, AdjP, AdvP, PP

### 3. Immediate Constituent (IC) Analysis
- Principles and limitations of IC analysis

### 4. Generative Syntax & Phrase Structure
- Phrase Structure Rules (PSR)
- **X-Bar Theory:** Specifier, Head, Complement, Adjunct
- Transformational Rules: Movement operations (*Wh-movement, NP-movement*)
- Minimalist operations: **Merge**, **Move**, and **Adjunction**

### 5. Clause Structure & Types
- Finite vs. Non-finite clauses
- Clause elements: Subject, Verb, Object, Complement, Adverbial (SVOCA)

### 6. Grammatical Categories (Tangent)
- Tense, Aspect, Voice, and Mood

### 7. Contemporary Theories of Syntax
- **Government and Binding Theory (GB)**
- **Lexical-Functional Grammar (LFG)**
- **Relational Grammar (RG)**
- **Role and Reference Grammar (RRG)**
