## 🎯 Course Objective
Explores **Phonetics** (the physical properties of speech sounds: articulatory, acoustic, and auditory) and **Phonology** (how sounds function within the linguistic system).

---

## 🗣️ Syllabus Topics & Study Modules

### 1. Foundations: Phonetics vs. Phonology
- Articulatory, Acoustic, and Auditory phonetics
- Correlation between phonetic realization and phonological systems

### 2. Phonemes, Phones, and Allophones
- Phone, Phoneme, and Allophone definitions
- Contrastive distribution vs. Complementary distribution vs. Free variation
- Minimal pairs and phonetic environments
- **Phonological Theorists:**
  - *Nikolai Trubetzkoy* (Prague School)
  - *Roman Jakobson* (Distinctive features)
  - *Daniel Jones* (Cardinal Vowels)
  - *Noam Chomsky & Morris Halle* (SPE Generative Phonology)

### 3. English Vowels
- Cardinal vowel quadrilateral
- **Monophthongs (12 Pure Vowels):** Front, Central, Back; Close, Half-close, Half-open, Open
- **Diphthongs (8 Gliding Vowels):** Centring (*ɪə, eə, ʊə*) and Closing (*eɪ, aɪ, ɔɪ, əʊ, aʊ*)
- **Triphthongs (5):** *eɪə, aɪə, ɔɪə, əʊə, aʊə*

### 4. English Consonants
- Voicing, Place of Articulation, Manner of Articulation
- **Places of Articulation (8):** Bilabial, Labiodental, Dental, Alveolar, Post-alveolar, Palatal, Velar, Glottal
- **Manners of Articulation (6):** Plosive, Fricative, Affricate, Nasal, Lateral/Approximant, Semi-vowel (Glide)

### 5. The Syllable & Phonotactics
- Phonetic and phonological definitions
- Onset, Rhyme (Nucleus + Coda)
- Syllable weight: Light vs. Heavy syllables
- **Phonotactic possibilities:** Permissible consonant clusters (Initial CCC- and Final -CCCC clusters)

### 6. Connected Speech
- **Strong and Weak Forms**
- **Contractions**
- **Elision:** Vowels (*ə*) and consonants (*t, d*)
- **Assimilation:** Anticipatory, Perseverative, Coalescent (Place, Manner, Voice)
- **Linking:** Linking /r/, Intrusive /r/, glottal stop

### 7. Stress & Prominence
- Acoustic correlates: Pitch, loudness, length, quality
- Word stress rules (nouns, verbs, adjectives, compounds)
- Sentence stress and rhythm: Stress-timed vs. Syllable-timed languages

### 8. Intonation & Tone
- Sense groups, tone units, and tonic syllables (nucleus)
- **Types of Tone:** Fall, Rise, Fall-Rise, Rise-Fall, Level
- **Functions:** Attitudinal, Grammatical, Accentual, Discourse

### 9. IPA Transcription
- Broad (phonemic) vs. Narrow (allophonic) transcription
- Practical transcription of connected English speech
